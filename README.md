# Multi-channel Audio Research Lab

## A shared space for experimental research

**What could you investigate if you had 24 independently addressable loudspeakers suspended from the ceiling?**

This repository is an open invitation to imagine, propose, and develop experiments that make unusual use of an existing physical installation.

The lab is **not dedicated to any one project, discipline, or technology**. Its purpose is to make a distinctive shared resource available for collaboration among faculty, students, and researchers. Some experiments might use artificial intelligence or real-time sensing; others might need nothing more than carefully programmed sound.

Our long-term ambition is a lively pipeline of proposals, collaborations, and experiments—enough interesting uses for the facility that researchers are eager to book time in it.

## Existing installation

- **24 suspended JBL loudspeakers** installed in the ceiling area.
- **Individually assignable audio channels:** each speaker can be assigned its own channel.

Additional specifications—including the speaker layout, model numbers, control system, routing, and room acoustics—will be documented as they are confirmed. Microphones, sensors, and particular AI systems are possibilities for future experiments, not confirmed installed equipment.

## A starting catalogue of research possibilities

These examples are **invitations to explore**, not an approved project list or a claim that the necessary software or sensors already exist.

| Field | Possible research questions or experiences |
| --- | --- |
| **Social psychology** | Could adaptive audience laughter or other social sounds influence participation, perceived group norms, or the willingness to speak? |
| **Cognitive psychology** | How do sound location, movement, competing sources, or spatial cues affect attention, memory, and task performance? |
| **Education and learning sciences** | Can sound cues support class participation, collaborative work, or transitions between lively discussion and quiet concentration? |
| **Psychoacoustics and perception** | How accurately do listeners locate, track, or separate sounds produced across many physical channels? |
| **Music and performance** | What new compositions, improvisations, or interactive performances become possible when sound moves among 24 speakers? |
| **Human–computer interaction** | How do people understand, respond to, and control an adaptive acoustic environment? |
| **AI and machine learning** | Can software learn audio strategies through measured feedback, or coordinate multiple independently controlled channels? |
| **Multi-agent systems** | Could independent agents cooperate, compete, or communicate through spatially distributed audio? |
| **Engineering and signal processing** | What can be demonstrated or measured through beamforming, interference, localization, or channel coordination, within the installation's physical limits? |
| **Accessibility and inclusive design** | Can spatial audio convey useful directional or navigational information, and for whom does it help or hinder? |
| **Art, design, and storytelling** | How might immersive narratives, installations, and interactive soundscapes use the room itself as part of the experience? |

### Illustrative project: An adaptive audience

Imagine an experimental *adaptive laugh track*. With microphones or other appropriate sensors added, software could estimate a group's response and adjust sound on individual speaker channels. Researchers could compare fixed playback, responsive playback, and no playback to test effects on laughter, participation, comfort, and perceived social influence.

The aim is not to assume such an effect exists but to **formulate testable hypotheses** and discover what happens. This project might involve psychologists designing the study, computer science students implementing the system, and education researchers examining its applicability to classrooms.

## How collaboration could work

The facility offers a common physical platform, while collaborators bring different questions and expertise.

- **Faculty and researchers** propose hypotheses, study designs, or artistic investigations.
- **Students** build software, run experiments, collect data, and analyze results.
- **Cross-disciplinary teams** combine subject-area expertise with audio engineering, computing, and experimental methods.
- **Courses and independent projects** use the same installation in different ways, reusing documented tools and lessons from previous projects.

A project need not require AI, use every speaker, or invent new hardware to belong here. Small, well-defined experiments are as welcome as ambitious ones.

### Propose an experiment

A useful proposal might include:

1. **Question:** What do you want to learn, demonstrate, or create?
2. **Use of the installation:** Why are multiple independently addressable speakers useful?
3. **Setup:** What additional software, sensors, or equipment would be needed?
4. **Method:** What would participants or systems do, and what outcomes would you measure?
5. **Collaborators:** Which skills or disciplines could help?
6. **Practical considerations:** How much room time, preparation, and review might be needed?

A GitHub issue can serve as the starting point for discussing an idea. As the project grows, its protocol, code, findings, and documentation can live in the repository or linked project repositories.

Human-participant research should include appropriate informed consent, privacy and accessibility considerations, safe audio exposure, and institutional ethics review where required. Research into influencing behavior should be transparent to participants rather than covert manipulation.

## Building a research commons

The measure of success is not a single flagship experiment. It is a growing community of people who recognize a new opportunity in the installation, collaborate across specialties, and leave behind tools or findings that make the **next** project easier.

This README is a starting invitation. The possibilities should expand as more researchers encounter the space and ask questions nobody has proposed yet.
